THE BUYING GUIDE · START WITH YOUR FORMAT

A turntable is one part of a DJ system.

Choose a deck for the way you will use it—scratch practice, a vinyl-and-digital setup, or playing records at home. Then budget for the mixer, cartridge and connections that make that choice work. A low deck price is not a complete-rig price.

THE REST OF THE SIGNAL CHAIN

Count the whole system before you compare prices.

For ordinary two-deck mixing, plan for two decks and the supporting items below. One deck may suit listening, sampling or learning controls; it does not provide two live sources for a conventional two-deck blend.

Items that change your true total

  • Deck count: two for a conventional two-deck vinyl mix; one for single-deck uses.
  • Cartridge + stylus: confirm what ships in the box and whether it suits your use.
  • Mixer / preamp: phono inputs and ground posts, or a suitable external phono stage.
  • Monitoring: headphones for cueing; speakers plus any required amp for the room.
  • Small essentials: slipmats, spare stylus, RCA / ground leads, record brush and a stable surface.
  • DVS, if needed: compatible software tier, timecode records, and a licensed mixer or audio interface.

BEFORE YOU ORDER

Three checks that prevent expensive mismatches.

01 / SIGNAL

PHONO or LINE?

Confirm whether the deck’s output is set to PHONO or LINE and which matching input your mixer has. A connector that fits is not enough.

02 / SOFTWARE

Which DVS license?

Timecode records alone do not unlock DVS. Confirm your software tier, mixer/interface support, and current OS/version with the software maker.

03 / CONDITION

What exactly is included?

Used listings may omit a cartridge, dust cover, hinges, ground lead or original box. Ask for a test and written condition notes.

Turntable buying questions

Do I need one turntable or two?

One is enough for listening, sampling, digitizing or a single-deck learning setup. A conventional two-source vinyl mix needs two turntables and a mixer with enough channels.

Do all DJ turntables need a phono preamp?

A PHONO-level output needs a phono input on the mixer or a separate phono preamp. Some turntables include a switchable preamp and LINE output; verify the specific model and setting, then connect it to a LINE input.

Can I use any turntable for DVS?

A suitable turntable is only one part of a DVS setup. You also need the correct timecode records and a compatible software license plus a supported mixer or audio interface. Confirm those exact combinations with the software maker before buying.

Should I buy a used Technics SL-1200MK2?

It can be a sensible used purchase when its condition is verified and the price includes any likely service or stylus replacement. Check the individual unit’s pitch control, tonearm, cueing, ground and outputs. The photograph on this page is an SL-1200MK2, not an MK7.

Is a built-in USB port the same as DVS?

No. USB can provide a computer audio connection for a supported workflow or help digitize records, depending on the model. DVS specifically requires timecode playback and software / mixer or interface support.
