Learn to beatmatch in the browser

Two synthesised records and one job: get them playing as one. Four short lessons take tempo and phase apart, hand them back together, then switch the readouts off — with the milliseconds measured instead of guessed at.

Reading about beatmatching and doing it are different skills, and most people practise in the wrong order: they try to learn inside a real mix, where one bad nudge costs them the blend and a good one teaches them nothing because they never find out what was wrong. The mix lab separates the job into the order it is actually done: find the tempo, line up the beats, hold the blend.

Deck A is a fixed four-bar house loop — the record already in the room. Deck B is a second loop in the same key on a different drum machine, and it arrives slightly out of tempo, slightly out of position, or both, depending on the lesson. Both are generated live in your browser, so nothing loads and nothing is recorded. The fourth lesson does the honest thing and takes the readouts away.

Because the page knows where both kicks are, it can tell you something your ear cannot yet: exactly how far apart they were, in milliseconds, and whether the gap is closing or opening.

How the controls work, and the order to use them
  1. 1Tempo before phase. If the gap between kicks keeps growing or shrinking, the pitch fader is wrong. Do not nudge until the gap stops moving.
  2. 2Then shift the beat. Nudge or drag the platter in tiny steps; a correction bigger than the error makes a new error. The Drop on beat button snaps Deck B's next kick onto Deck A's grid, which is what the cue button does on real gear.
  3. 3Hold it. A blend is not a moment you hit and forget — it is a state you keep for the length of the transition.

What the lab is actually training

Two separate skills, and the lab keeps them separate on purpose:

  • Tempo matching is noticing that the gap between two kicks is changing over time. It is slow feedback: you hear it over four, eight, sixteen bars.
  • Phase correction is noticing that the kicks are in the wrong place relative to each other, right now. It is fast feedback: you hear it immediately.

Almost everyone who "cannot beatmatch" is correcting phase constantly while their tempo is still wrong. The result is a permanent chase — you nudge, it drifts back, you nudge again. Lesson 1 makes that impossible to miss: the kicks start together and then slide apart on their own. No amount of nudging fixes a tempo error, and no pitch fader fixes a beat that is simply in the wrong place.

What each lesson is really teaching you

The lessons are not difficulty levels, they are the parts of one move, in the order the move is made. The fourth removes the training wheels on purpose.

A controller's pitch fader moves about 0.08% per millimetre on a ±8% range, so a 0.05% correction is roughly half a millimetre of travel. That is the size of move the drill is asking for — which is why the fader here is scaled to the same ±6–8% span rather than a full record-player octave.

Taking it to real gear

The lab removes the two things that make real practice hard: you do not have to find two records that fit, and a mistake costs nothing. When all four lessons are done, switch to free practice — no goal, no hint, no result card — and hold a blend for a full minute. Then move to your controller and repeat it with two copies of one track: pitch only for the first minute, phase second.

Questions about the mix lab

Can you learn to beatmatch without DJ equipment?

Yes. Beatmatching is a listening skill, and this lab trains the same ear you use on a controller. What it cannot teach is the feel of a jog wheel and the cue/monitor workflow — that needs hardware, and the guide covers it.

Why do the two decks sound different?

So you can tell them apart while they overlap. Both loops sit in A minor and follow the same four-chord progression, so a correct blend is consonant and a wrong one flams — but Deck A uses a rounder kick, offbeat hats and a snare, while Deck B uses a tighter kick, shuffled shakers and an offbeat pluck. Identical loops would let you lock them by accident and never know.

Do I need any of the readouts to learn?

No, and they are not the point. The offset meter and the beat grid exist so you can check yourself while you are still learning what "wrong" sounds like — lesson 4 switches them off entirely and only shows the numbers once you have held the blend. After that, free practice is the place to mix with your eyes shut.

Does this work on a phone?

Yes — tap the nudge buttons, drag the pitch fader, and drag the platter to shift the beat. Headphones make the detail much easier to hear, especially the flam between the two kicks; phone speakers cannot reproduce it.

Is anything recorded?

No. The audio is generated by your browser, your progress is a single localStorage flag per lesson, and nothing leaves the page.
