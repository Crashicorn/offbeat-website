DDJ-400 vs DDJ-FLX4 · UPDATED 26 OCTOBER 2026

Two channels each. The meaningful changes are software, USB and workflow.

The DDJ-400 and DDJ-FLX4 share more than their familiar club-style layout: both are two-channel controllers with a built-in audio interface and an RCA master output. The archived DDJ-400 is listed for PC/Mac software; the current FLX4 also supports selected mobile app modes. This comparison sticks to published product facts and the differences that change a real setup—no numeric quality scores or pretend lab testing.

Buying new?

Start with the DDJ-FLX4 if USB-C, Smart Fader/Smart CFX and the currently listed software options fit your plan.

Considering a used DDJ-400?

It can still suit a rekordbox or djay workflow. Check the unit, included cable, software/OS support and seller terms first; its price varies by condition.

Need four channels, XLR master out or standalone play?

Neither model meets that requirement. Compare a larger controller or a standalone system instead.

Software support: don't mix up an app name and a license tier

Pioneer lists rekordbox, Serato DJ Lite, Traktor Play and djay for the FLX4. Native Instruments separately documents plug-and-play support for Traktor Pro 4.4.0 and later, so do not treat Play and Pro as the same edition or assume the controller unlocks a Pro license. Serato DJ Pro and djay Pro are paid tiers. Check the current operating-system, app-version and microphone-routing details before purchase.

The archived DDJ-400 is less straightforward. Pioneer lists rekordbox and djay, but its support article says the DDJ-400 is not compatible with Serato, while Serato maintains a DDJ-400 hardware page. Because those public sources do not agree, we do not treat Serato compatibility as guaranteed for a used unit. Confirm directly with both vendors and do not rely on a third-party mapping for paid work.

What changes in a real beginner setup?

CHOOSE THE FLX4

You are buying new and need a current software path

USB-C and Smart Fader/Smart CFX are concrete generation changes. Its two-channel layout and RCA master still fit a straightforward laptop, headphones and powered-speaker setup.

CONSIDER THE DDJ-400

The used price and condition make sense

The familiar two-deck workflow and built-in audio interface are still useful. Inspect the jogs, faders, USB socket, included cable and software/OS support; compare the true used cost with a current controller.

LOOK BEYOND BOTH

Your setup needs more than two channels

For four-channel mixing, balanced master outputs, booth outputs or laptop-free operation, compare hardware designed for that need rather than adapting either entry controller.

Make the speaker connection part of the decision

Both controllers provide an RCA master output. For a compact powered-monitor pair with RCA inputs, run the controller's left/right RCA outputs to the pair's left/right inputs; headphones connect to the controller for cueing. If your target speaker takes only balanced XLR/TRS, choose the correct cable and keep the unbalanced run short—an adapter does not turn the output into a balanced one.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which should I buy: DDJ-400 or DDJ-FLX4?

Choose by condition, current software support and connection needs. The FLX4 is the current model with USB-C, Smart Fader/Smart CFX and a current software list. The DDJ-400 can still suit a verified used setup at a meaningfully lower total price. Both are two-channel controllers with built-in audio interfaces.

Is the DDJ-FLX4 a four-channel controller?

No. The DDJ-FLX4 is a two-channel, two-deck controller, as is the DDJ-400. If you need four-channel mixing, compare a controller designed for four channels or a standalone system.

Does the DDJ-400 work with Serato?

Public vendor sources conflict: AlphaTheta's DDJ-400 support article says it is not compatible with Serato, while Serato hosts a DDJ-400 hardware page. Check directly with both vendors for your exact software version and operating system before buying a used unit.

Do both controllers have a built-in audio interface?

Yes. Both include a built-in audio interface. The FLX4's USB-C connection is a generation change, but an audio interface is not unique to it.

Does the DDJ-FLX4 support Traktor?

Yes, in two distinct ways: Pioneer lists Traktor Play, and Native Instruments offers FLX4 owners a free Play copy through Native Access. Native Instruments separately documents plug-and-play support for Traktor Pro 4.4.0 and later. Play is the free streamlined tier; it is not a Pro license. Traktor Pro microphone input may need manual routing, and some Windows 11 setups have documented audio workarounds, so check NI’s current OS and setup notes.
